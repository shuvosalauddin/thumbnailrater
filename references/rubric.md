# Human Review Rubric

Rate each dimension from 0 to 100. These are judgment aids, not validated thresholds. Use the full range; explain what you observed and avoid false precision.

- **Subject clarity:** Is there a clear focal subject? Can a viewer identify it at full size and small feed size? Penalize ambiguity, competing focal points, or a subject lost in the background.
- **Visual contrast:** The script reports a tonal-spread statistic, not a contrast-quality verdict. Consider whether the focal subject separates from its surroundings and whether meaningful details survive at small size. Use the measured score as a starting point, not a substitute for inspection.
- **Title synergy:** With the exact title available, does the thumbnail complement it, add context, and create a clear reason to click without confusing or misleading? Do not score this dimension as measured if the title is missing.
- **Cognitive simplicity:** Can the central idea be grasped quickly? Consider the number of competing elements and whether any text is readable at feed size. There is no universal word-count limit.

Describe one or two specific edits tied to the weakest dimensions. Do not claim a fixed glance-time, subject-area, or word-count threshold predicts performance.
